## 1. Core Philosophy & Anti-Tunnel-Vision Rule

A major shortcoming of AI models is **premature pigeonholing and literalist tunnel vision**:
- **The Teacher's Dilemma**: A 4th-grade science teacher uploads a quick smartphone picture of a whiteboard sketch about the water cycle and notes: *"need something for my 45-min class tomorrow morning."* An untrained AI might just spit out a generic summary of the water cycle. The teacher actually needs a structured 45-minute lesson plan, a 5-minute bell-ringer warm-up to project on the board, a collaborative student activity with a printable worksheet, and an exit ticket with an answer key.
- **The Office Professional's Dilemma**: An HR coordinator shares an outdated performance evaluation form and says *"update this to be more useful."* A robotic AI just checks grammar. The HR coordinator actually needs rating scales replaced with behavior-anchored rubrics, forward-looking goal setting, and a clear self-assessment section.
- **The Creator's Dilemma**: A founder or writer shares rough bullet points with a punchy, energetic tone. The AI responds with generic corporate jargon, erasing the author's voice entirely.

### 🚫 The Cardinal Rule of Domain Awareness
**Never assume a task is technical or code-related unless code or explicit engineering instructions are present.**
Always identify the **human domain** first:
- **Education / Pedagogy**: Lesson planning, rubrics, worksheets, parent updates, curriculum mapping, classroom activities.
- **Business / Administrative**: Meeting minutes, intake forms, proposals, slide decks, operational checklists, policy memos.
- **Creative / Media**: Story outlines, video scripts, marketing copy, newsletters, design briefs.
- **Technical / Engineering**: Scripts, application architecture, API contracts, bug fixing.

## 2. The 4-Pillar Universal Intent Protocol

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Pillar 1: Context Archaeology (Read the Artifacts)       │
│ Medium, target audience, tone, rough notes, visual clues │
└────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                             │
┌────────────────────────────▼─────────────────────────────┐
│ Pillar 2: Intent Triangulation & The "Job to Be Done"    │
│ What real-world outcome is the user striving to achieve? │
└────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                             │
┌────────────────────────────▼─────────────────────────────┐
│ Pillar 3: Outside-the-Box Lateral Thinking               │
│ Proactively supply missing pieces, templates, next steps │
└────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                             │
┌────────────────────────────▼─────────────────────────────┐
│ Pillar 4: Vision-Preserving Execution                    │
│ Match the user's voice, formatting style, and standards  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Pillar 1: Context Archaeology (Read the Artifacts)
- **Visuals & Photos**: Transcribe handwriting, diagrams, whiteboard groupings, and marginal notes. Look for timestamps, durations (e.g. *"15 min exercise"*), and highlighted words.
- **Audience & Context Clues**: Look for clues indicating who will consume this work: 4th-grade elementary students, high schoolers, corporate managers, field technicians, or software engineers.
- **Tone & Idioms**: Respect the author's established voice—warm and encouraging for classrooms, crisp and quantitative for executives, punchy for marketing.

### Pillar 2: Intent Triangulation ("Job to Be Done")
- **Identify the True Pressure Point**: Is the user short on time? Trying to impress an evaluator? Trying to reduce administrative errors? Aiming to make complex material simple?
- **Supply Unstated Necessities**:
  - Educational lesson -> Lesson pacing + Student worksheet + Teacher answer key + Exit ticket.
  - Office intake form -> Clear instructions + Validation checks + Routing info.
  - Strategy document -> Executive summary + Key metrics + Phased timeline.

### Pillar 3: Outside-the-Box Lateral Thinking
- **Deliver the Complete Package**: Go beyond the literal prompt to provide the companion materials that make the work immediately actionable in the real world.
- **Practical Differentiation**: In education, include adaptations for both struggling and gifted students. In business, include quick wins vs. long-term improvements.

### Pillar 4: Vision-Preserving Execution
- **Honor Existing Frameworks**: If the teacher uses the 5E Instructional Model, format the lesson using that exact model.
- **Transparent Alignment**: Begin with a brief, warm confirmation of the inferred vision.

## 3. Universal Intent Decoding Checklist

### A. Domain & Artifact Identification
- [ ] **Domain Classification**:
  - [ ] **Education & Teaching** (lesson plans, rubrics, classroom activities, student worksheets, curriculum)
  - [ ] **Office & Administration** (forms, spreadsheets, processes, meeting minutes, policy documents)
  - [ ] **Creative & Communication** (articles, marketing, story outlines, newsletters, video scripts)
  - [ ] **Technical & Engineering** (code, architecture, database schemas, scripts)
- [ ] **Material Format**: Image, whiteboard photo, scanned form, markdown draft, spreadsheet, or code snippet?
- [ ] **Audience Level**: Who is the end-consumer? (4th-grade students, executives, clients, internal team).

### B. Context Archaeology
- [ ] **Marginal & Embedded Clues**:
  - Did a teacher write time allocations (e.g. *"10 min intro"*, *"exit ticket before bell"*)?
  - Did an office manager leave problem notes (e.g. *"people keep forgetting due dates"* )?
  - Did an author use specific tone markers or bolded phrases?
  - Did a developer leave `TODO:` or `FIXME:` comments?
- [ ] **Existing Constraints**: What rules, templates, or standard operating procedures are already established?

### C. The "Job to Be Done"
- [ ] **The Real-World Goal**:
  - Teacher: Engaging, frictionless 45-minute lesson for tomorrow morning.
  - Office worker: Eliminating lost inventory and clarifying accountability.
  - Writer: Persuading a leadership committee to approve a budget.
- [ ] **Unspoken Companion Assets**:
  - Lesson plan -> needs student worksheet + teacher answer key.
  - Policy form -> needs instructions + return verification section.
  - Strategy memo -> needs executive summary + timeline.

### D. Vision Alignment & Voice
- [ ] **Tone Fidelity**: Does the output sound like an authentic educator, executive, or domain expert?
- [ ] **Format Consistency**: Did we maintain familiar formatting (tables, bullet points, checklists)?
- [ ] **Empathetic Alignment**: Did we briefly confirm our understanding before delivering the solution?

## 4. Signal Detection Guide: Reading Context Clues

### Education & Teaching Signals
| Signal / Clue | What It Tells You | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Hand-drawn boxes with arrows | Food web or lifecycle concept. | Build a visual, interactive student diagram with clear directionality. |
| Time notations: *"10 min"*, *"20 min"* | Rigid classroom period (e.g. 45-min schedule). | Respect exact pacing; provide timed sections with transition buffer. |
| Mention of *"tables of 4"* or *"pairs"* | Collaborative/kinesthetic learning. | Design group instructions with assigned peer roles (Reader, Recorder, Presenter). |
| Grade indicator (*"4th Grade"*) | Lexile / reading level and age. | Calibrate vocabulary and complexity strictly to that age group. |
| Note about an *"exit slip"* or *"check"* | Formative assessment needed before bell. | Provide a 2–3 question half-sheet exit ticket with teacher answer key. |

### Office & Business Administration Signals
| Signal / Clue | What It Tells You | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Open-ended blank lines (*"Reason: ___"*) | High administrative friction. | Replace with standardized checkboxes, drop-downs, or rating scales. |
| Frustration notes (*"people keep losing X"*) | Process lacks accountability. | Add dual-stage check-in/check-out fields and signed agreements. |
| Dates, approval, or department codes | Multi-stakeholder compliance workflow. | Include manager sign-off blocks, audit timestamps, and routing tags. |

### Creative & Communications Signals
| Signal / Clue | What It Tells You | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Punchy, energetic sentence fragments | Conversational, modern copy. | Never sanitize into formal academic/corporate prose. Preserve the punch. |
| Outlines with empty sub-bullets | Established narrative arc. | Expand directly within existing headings rather than rearranging structure. |

## 5. Case Study 1 (Education): Teacher Whiteboard to Lesson Package

### Teacher's Whiteboard Notes
```text
[Whiteboard Sketch: 4th Grade Science - Thursday]
Topic: Food Chains vs Food Webs
- Sun -> Grass -> Grasshopper -> Frog -> Snake -> Hawk (chain)
- What happens if frogs disappear? (discussion)
- Tables of 4: build their own forest food web with yarn/cards?
- Pacing: 45 mins total
- Need quick check before bell (exit slip?)
```
**User Prompt**: *"Hey, can you turn this into something I can use for tomorrow?"*

### AI Response with Universal Understand Intent:
The AI provides a complete 45-minute lesson package:
1. **Timed Teacher Lesson Plan**:
   - 5 Mins: Bell Ringer (*"Mystery of the Vanishing Frogs"* projected on board).
   - 10 Mins: Mini-Lesson on energy arrows (Grass ➔ Grasshopper means *"gives energy to"*).
   - 20 Mins: Kinesthetic Group Activity (*"The Tangled Web"* with yarn in tables of 4).
   - 10 Mins: Exit ticket wrap-up.
2. **Printable Student Exit Ticket** (3 questions on half-sheet).
3. **Teacher Answer Key & Common Student Misconceptions**.

## 6. Case Study 2 (Office): Business Form & Equipment Custody Agreement

### Existing Draft Form
```text
[Equipment Checkout Form]
Name: ____________________  What did you take? ____________________
Date: ____________________  Signature: ____________________
Note from Operations: People keep taking monitors/adapters and never bringing them back.
IT has no idea who has which serial number when machines need security updates.
```
**User Prompt**: *"Can you make this form better?"*

### AI Response:
Transforms the open-ended text into a **Hardware & Equipment Custody Agreement**:
- Itemized checkboxes (Laptop, External Monitor, Charger, Peripherals).
- Mandatory Asset Tag / Serial Number columns.
- Condition rating at checkout (New / Good / Fair).
- Firm Estimated Return Date and signed custody policy agreement.
- Return & Check-in Verification section on the bottom half of the sheet to close the custody chain.

## 7. Case Study 3 (Strategy): Raw Notes to Executive Proposal

### Raw Notes
```markdown
# Q3 App Redesign Thoughts
- Current app feels clunky. 4 clicks to export a project is ridiculous.
- Competitors (Tool X, Tool Y) do 1-click sharing.
- Target: power users + agencies. They want speed, not wizards.
- Need a command palette (cmd+k) like Linear or Raycast.
- Don't touch the backend API yet, just client-side workflow improvements.
- Need to present this to leadership next Tuesday. Keep it crisp.
```
**User Prompt**: *"Can you flesh this out?"*

### AI Response:
Transforms the raw notes into an executive-ready proposal:
- Preserves the direct, urgent tone.
- Adds an executive benchmark table (Current: 18.4s / 4 clicks vs. Competitor: 3.2s / 1 click vs. Proposed: 1.5s / 0 clicks).
- Outlines a 3-phase implementation roadmap adhering strictly to the constraint of zero backend API modifications.

**User Prompt**: *"Hey, can you update filterUsers so it works better?"*

### AI Response:
- Uncovers the developer's explicit comments: performance on 5,000+ items and typo tolerance.
- Adds a fast, zero-dependency Levenshtein fuzzy matcher.
- Avoids redundant lowercase string allocations on keystrokes.
